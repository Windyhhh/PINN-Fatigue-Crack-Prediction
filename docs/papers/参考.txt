# A physics-informed neural network method for identifying parameters and predicting remaining life of fatigue crack growth
**Authors**: Wangwang Liao, Xiangyun Long*, Chao Jiang*  
**Affiliation**: College of Mechanical and Vehicle Engineering, Hunan University, Changsha City, PR China  
**Journal**: International Journal of Fatigue 191 (2025) 108678  
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2024.108678  
**Publication Information**: Received 14 June 2024; Revised 13 August 2024; Accepted 29 October 2024; Available online 30 October 2024  

## Keywords
Fatigue crack growth; Remaining life assessment; Physics-informed neural networks; Data-physics fusion; Parameter identification

## Abstract
Predicting the remaining life of fatigue cracks is crucial for planning maintenance and repair strategies to prevent untoward incidents. This paper proposes a novel physics-informed neural network (PINN) method for identifying parameters and predicting remaining fatigue crack growth life (FCGL). Initially, the relationship between crack length and fatigue cycles is established through a neural network, and the gradient of fatigue cycles with respect to crack length is obtained by automatic differentiation. Subsequently, a composite loss function is designed to incorporate this gradient within the confines of physical knowledge, ensuring that the established relationship not only aligns with observed data but also adheres to physical knowledge. Furthermore, during the network training, the parameters in physical models are simultaneously updated to better conform to the individuality of the monitored subject. All predicted remaining FCGLs fall within the 1.5 times error band. Compared to purely data-driven or physics-based methods, the proposed method offers more robust and accurate predictions of remaining FCGLs.

## 1. Introduction
Fatigue cracks frequently occur in materials or structures due to cyclic loadings [1–3]. Their propagation can lead to fracture failure, causing accidents or endangering structural safety. Remaining fatigue crack growth life (FCGL) refers to the life of FCG from an initial size to a critical size under fatigue cyclic loading. Accurate prediction is crucial for planning maintenance strategies (repairs, replacements, reinforcements) to prevent untoward incidents.

### 1.1 Existing Methods
- **Physics-based methods**: Based on experimental analyses and theoretical investigations of FCG [4–10]. The Paris law [10] (linear elastic fracture mechanics) is widely used, revealing a power-law relationship between fatigue crack growth rate (FCGR) and stress intensity factor (SIF) range. Extended research incorporates complex loading [11,12], nonlinear material behaviors [13–16], and uncertainties [17].
- **Data-driven methods**: Promoted by computing technology, they approximate fatigue damage processes without presupposing functional forms. Advantages include easy integration of multiple factors (materials, load, defects) [21], and applications in fatigue life prediction [22–24], defect detection [25–27], and SIF calculation [28,29]. Limitations: require large labeled data (costly/difficult to acquire), "black box" issues (poor interpretability, extrapolation, overfitting) [30–31].

### 1.2 Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN)
PINN integrates physical knowledge/empirical formulas into neural networks, enabling high-precision predictions with limited data [32–33]. Hot topic in fatigue and fracture [34–37], with three fusion types:
1. Use physical model predictions as inputs or fuse outputs [38].
2. Construct physics-enriched network structures (e.g., Paris law-based RNN for damage accumulation) [35,39].
3. Incorporate physical information into loss functions (empirical models, S-N curves, constitutive relationships) [40–42].

### 1.3 Research Gap & Innovation
Little attention to PINN-based remaining FCGL prediction, which relies on limited prior data. Existing methods have limitations: physical models face parameter identification challenges with limited data; data-driven methods lack interpretability and extrapolation. This paper proposes a PINN method integrating FCG data and physical knowledge, constraining network gradients via physical laws and inversely identifying parameters during training.

## 2. Overview of the Proposed Method
### 2.1 Objective
Comprehensively utilize FCG physical knowledge and observed data to identify physical model parameters, enhance extrapolation capability, and accurately predict remaining FCGL from early-stage crack length (a) and fatigue cycles (n) data.

### 2.2 Framework (Fig. 1)
1. **Data & Knowledge Preparation**: Input (a) → Output (n); physical knowledge from Paris law [10].
2. **PINN Construction**: Embed physical knowledge via composite loss function.
3. **Training**: Train network with observed data, update Paris law parameters simultaneously.
4. **Validation**: Verify performance with two FCG datasets.

## 3. Methodology (Fig. 2)
### 3.1 Physical Knowledge of Remaining FCGL
- **SIF (Stress Intensity Factor)**: Describes crack growth driving force (linear elastic fracture mechanics [7]); larger SIF → stronger crack tip stress field → easier propagation.
- **Paris Law**: Relationship between FCGR and SIF range (\(\Delta K\)):  
  \[\frac{da}{dn}=C(\Delta K)^m\]  
  where C, m = material parameters; \(\Delta K\) = SIF range (analytical calculation for simple specimens, numerical for complex geometries).
- **Remaining FCGL Calculation**: Integrate Paris law:  
  \[n_{Ref}=\int_{a_t}^{a_c} \frac{1}{C(\Delta K)^m}da\]  
  where \(a_t\) = current crack length; \(a_c\) = critical failure crack length.

### 3.2 Artificial Neural Networks
- **Structure**: 3 perceptron layers (mitigate overfitting for limited data), output:  
  \[n=NN(a)=\pmb{w}^{(3)}\left(f\left(w^{(2)}\left(f\left(w^{(1)}a+b^{(1)}\right)\right)+b^{(2)}\right)\right)+b^{(3)}\]  
  where \(w^{(i)}, b^{(i)}\) = weights/biases (i=1,2,3); \(f(\cdot)\) = activation function.
- **Activation Function**: Tanh (nonlinearity, smoothness, non-zero second derivative for easy training):  
  \[tanh(x)=\frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{e^x + e^{-x}}\]

### 3.3 Loss Functions Incorporating Physical Knowledge
Total loss = data loss (\(Loss_D\)) + physical loss (\(Loss_P\)) (both MSELoss):  
\[
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
Loss_D=MSELoss(n_{pred}, n_{obs}) \\
Loss_P=MSELoss\left(\frac{da}{dn}, C(\Delta K)^m\right) \\
Loss=\lambda_1 Loss_D + \lambda_2 Loss_P
\end{array}
\right.
\]  
where \(n_{pred}\) = model output; \(n_{obs}\) = observed cycles; \(\lambda_1, \lambda_2\) = hyperparameters (balance data/physical constraints).

- **Automatic Differentiation**: Compute gradient \(\frac{dn}{da}=\frac{\partial NN}{\partial a}\) (avoids tedious/error-prone manual calculation), substitute into \(Loss_P\):  
  \[Loss_P=MSELoss\left(\frac{1}{\frac{\partial NN}{\partial a}}, C(\Delta K)^m\right)\]

- **Optimization**: Minimize loss to update network parameters (w, b) and physical parameters (C, m):  
  \[w, b, C, m=arg\ min_{w,b,C,m} Loss(w,b,C,m)\]  
  Optimizers: SGD [46], Adam [47].

### 3.4 Parameters Identification
- **Individualized Parameters**: C and m vary with material microstructure; identified via PINN using observed data.
- **Training Procedure (Table 1, Algorithm 1)**:
  1. Input early observed data (\(a_{obs}, n_{obs}\)).
  2. Predict \(n_{pred}\) via NN, compute \(Loss_D\).
  3. Randomly sample hypothetical crack lengths \(a_p\) in \([a_0, a_c]\) (\(a_0\) = initial crack length).
  4. Compute network gradient \(\frac{\partial NN}{\partial a_p}\) via automatic differentiation.
  5. Calculate \(Loss_P\) and total Loss.
  6. Update parameters via backpropagation until convergence.
- **Remaining FCGL Prediction**:  
  \[n_{Ref}=NN(a_c) - n_t\]  
  where \(n_t\) = cycles at current observation moment.

## 4. Results and Analysis
Two datasets validate the method: Virkler et al. [49] (MT specimens) and Wu & Ni [50] (CT specimens). Training data = initial 20% of a-n curves; prediction data = remaining 80%.

### 4.1 2024-T3 Aluminum Alloy MT Specimens
- **Dataset**: 68 specimens (width=152.4mm, thickness=2.54mm); sinusoidal load (20Hz, \(F_{max}=5.25kN\), \(F_{min}=1.05kN\)); crack length \(a\): 9.00–49.80mm; 164 data points/specimen, 68 independent a-n curves.
- **\(\Delta K\) Calculation**:  
  \[\Delta K=\frac{\Delta F}{B}\sqrt{\frac{\pi \alpha}{2W}sec\frac{\pi \alpha}{2}}\]  
  where \(\Delta F=F_{max}-F_{min}\); W=specimen width; B=thickness; \(\alpha=2a/W\).
- **Hyperparameters (Table 2)**: Train ratio=0.2; epochs=25,000; optimizer=Adam; weight decay=0.001; learning rate=0.001; \(\lambda_1=100\), \(\lambda_2=1\); hidden layers=64×64; activation=Tanh; \(a_p\) per epoch=500.
- **Training Efficiency**: 1.5 min/25,000 epochs (NVIDIA GTX1650).
- **Loss Convergence (Fig. 4)**: \(Loss_P\) converges at ~18,000 epochs; valid loss approaches zero.

#### 4.1.1 Method Comparison (Fig. 5)
- **Data-driven method [20]**: Overfits training data, poor extrapolation (cumulative errors).
- **Physics-based method (Paris law)**: Better than data-driven but suffers from parameter deviation (linear fitting of early noisy data).
- **Proposed PINN**: Lowest prediction deviation across specimens.

#### 4.1.2 lg(da/dn)-lg(ΔK) Curve (Fig. 6)
- PINN’s nonlinear fitting of a-n data (vs. physics-based linear fitting of lg(da/dn)-lg(ΔK)) avoids conversion errors, yielding more accurate C and m.

#### 4.1.3 Prediction Accuracy (Fig. 7)
- PINN: All predictions within 1.5× dispersion band; 55.9% within 15% error.
- Physics-based: Mostly within 2.5× dispersion band; 17.7% within 15% error.

#### 4.1.4 Sensitivity Analysis (Fig. 8)
- L2RE (L2 relative error) decreases with more training data for both methods.
- PINN has lower/stable L2RE than physics-based method, especially with scarce data (10–20% training data).
- Physics-based method needs 40% training data (≈70% fatigue life) to match PINN’s performance with 20% data.

### 4.2 2024-T351 Aluminum Alloy CT Specimens
- **Dataset**: 30 specimens (W=50.0mm, B=12.0mm); sinusoidal load (15Hz, \(F_{max}=4.5kN\), \(F_{min}=0.9kN\)); crack length \(a\): 18mm to fracture; 30 repeated experiments.
- **\(\Delta K\) Calculation**:  
  \[\Delta K=\frac{\Delta P}{B\sqrt{W}}\frac{2+\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^{3/2}}\left(0.886+4.64\alpha-13.32\alpha^2+14.72\alpha^3-5.6\alpha^4\right)\]  
  where \(\alpha=a/W\).
- **Hyperparameters**: Same as MT specimens; C=2.83E-08, m=3.73 (initial values from validation data); n normalized by 10,000.
- **Loss Convergence (Fig. 10)**: All losses converge to near zero at 15,000 epochs.

#### 4.2.1 Method Comparison (Fig. 11)
- Data-driven and physics-based methods show increasing deviations with crack propagation.
- PINN maintains precise predictions across the entire a range.

#### 4.2.2 lg(da/dn)-lg(ΔK) Curve (Fig. 12)
- PINN’s iterative refinement via nonlinear mapping better reflects actual crack growth than physics-based method (susceptible to data fluctuations).

#### 4.2.3 Prediction Accuracy (Fig. 13)
- PINN: All predictions within 1.5× dispersion band; 83% within 15% error.
- Physics-based: Deviations within 2.5× dispersion band; 30% within 15% error.

## 5. Conclusion
- The proposed PINN method embeds FCG physical constraints into network gradients, enhancing prediction accuracy and interpretability.
- Physical parameters (C, m) are updateable, reflecting individualized crack propagation characteristics.
- Validated with two specimen types, showing simplicity and broad applicability (all predictions within 1.5× error band).

### Future Research Directions
- Incorporate uncertainties to improve prediction reliability.
- Explore advanced data fusion strategies.
- Handle complex crack growth scenarios.
- Integrate with digital twin for real-time monitoring and predictive maintenance.
